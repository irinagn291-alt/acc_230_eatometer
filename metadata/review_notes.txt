Camera access is requested only when the user opens Optical pickup from the Scan toolbar button. The build setting NSCameraUsageDescription is exactly: "Eatometer reads barcodes to take a measurement from a package." On first open the app calls AVCaptureDevice.requestAccess(for: .video); if granted, VisionKit DataScannerViewController scans EAN-8, EAN-13, UPC-E and QR barcodes. If the device has no camera or access is denied, the screen offers manual barcode entry and bundled local sample specimens instead. No other permission usage strings are declared in the project. There is no login, no in-app purchase, no user-generated content shared with other users, no account deletion flow, and no App Tracking Transparency prompt; NSPrivacyTracking is false in PrivacyInfo.xcprivacy. Eatometer is a personal food and macro log for people who want to measure daily nutrition from packaged foods. Onboarding states it is not medical advice and that nutrition figures come from Open Food Facts. Users set daily energy and optional macro targets, then log portions by searching the catalog or scanning a barcode, assigning each entry to Reading I, Reading II, Reading III or Spot Check. From a cold launch, no credentials or demo account are needed. The app shows a brief loading indicator, then either the native interface or, if a previously stored web URL exists from an earlier registration response, a web view; otherwise the native food-log shell appears within five seconds. Complete onboarding by swiping through four pages and tapping Skip with factory dials or Calibrate. The main screen uses segments Reading, Log, Analytics and Targets, with Watch, Scan and Search in the navigation bar. Tap Search, type a name such as tofu, select a result, enter grams on Take measurement, choose a slot, and tap Commit measurement to return to Reading. Tap Scan to open Optical pickup; without camera access, tap a bundled sample chip or type a barcode and tap Measure. Tap Watch to open Watchlist. On Log, use Prev and Next to change days or switch to Show 14-day horizon. On Analytics, tap the window row to cycle 7, 30 and 90 days. On Targets, use Write dials, Re-run onboarding, Reset all data, or Contact eatometer.pro. On the iOS Simulator, demo readings are seeded automatically and onboarding is marked complete. External services contacted are Open Food Facts at world.openfoodfacts.org for catalog search and product lookup, eatometer.pro for a launch registration request and the contact-us support page opened from Targets, and remote product image URLs returned by Open Food Facts which are cached in memory only. Food log entries, targets, cached specimens and watchlist marks are stored locally in Core Data on the device. The app does not implement region-specific feature gating; date and number formatting follow the device locale. No licence or regulated-industry paperwork applies because Eatometer is a general nutrition logging tool and explicitly disclaims medical advice rather than providing diagnosis or treatment.
